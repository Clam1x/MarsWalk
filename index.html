The app checks for a numeric HiRISE or CTX DTM covering both endpoints. Otherwise it routes on the HRSC/MOLA blend and refines intersecting sections with available stereo DTMs. Long routes may need a wider base sampling step. A stricter requirement stops the calculation if unavailable across the full route.

On native CTX/HiRISE terrain, the walking corridor checks measured forward and cross slope, apparent steps, missing cells and model edges. The step and clearance limits are adjustable assumptions; stereo noise can resemble a step, and features smaller than true DTM resolution remain unknown.

Detailed stereo terrain is checked for topographic depressions near the route. Approximate circles are planning cues, not measured crater rims. Coarse global terrain cannot resolve walking-scale craters; no detection means no claim of clearance.

This stricter mode requires one continuous HiRISE stereo DTM at 2 m or finer, readable HiRISE imagery along the whole route, and evidence for pixel provenance and image–terrain agreement. Missing quality evidence blocks approval. These checks cannot certify that walking is safe.

The HRSC guide is approximate. MOLA-base areas are resampled to 200 m without gaining that level of detail. CTX and HiRISE catalogue outlines can contain missing heights; a model join does not prove the physical slope. Review the data quality of every route section before using it for field planning.

Data sources

Numeric route heights: USGS HRSC/MOLA Blend DEM Global 200 m v2, fetched as small byte ranges from the original GeoTIFF. For routes contained in a local stereo model, the app reads numeric CTX DTMs or HiRISE DTMs from the USGS catalog. The preliminary global corridor and bundled overview use NASA MOLA MEGDR. The green area guide comes from the published HRSC footprint map; it is not a per-pixel provenance mask.

Images: NASA Mars Trek Viking VIS, Caltech global CTX mosaic, and HiRISE mosaic served by Esri. Photographs never replace numeric heights.

Place search uses a small IAU / USGS catalogue.

Mission forecast and communications assumptions

There is no validated live, surface-level temperature, wind, dust and radiation feed for every point on Mars. Mars Climate Database provides global model climatology, not a dated local forecast; MEDA provides historical measurements at the rover. Enter a mission forecast or measured scenario with its source; blank fields remain unknown. Direct radio range includes planetary curvature only.
